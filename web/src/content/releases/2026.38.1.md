---
date: 2026-09-14
title: What your sessions left running
summary: Wortel now shows the dev servers, builds and stray processes your sessions are still holding, what they cost, what they are serving, and a way to stop any of it. Including the ones whose session closed days ago.
---

**Wortel shows what each session is still running.** A new item appears in the toolbar when there is something to show, and opens a list grouped by session: one row per job, the port it is serving on, what it costs in memory, and a Quit that appears when you point at it. Click a row to open it in your browser, or right-click for Copy Address, Reveal in Finder and Quit. The item is absent, not greyed out, when nothing is running, so a terminal that has started nothing carries no extra chrome.

It reports memory the way Activity Monitor does, which turns out to matter more than it sounds. `ps` reported a live vite process at 30 MB while it was really holding 572 MB: on a machine under pressure, compressed and swapped pages leave the resident set, and the number every other tool shows you is the one that hides the problem. One session on the machine this was built against was holding 1.39 GB across two dev servers and an agent.

**It finds the ones nothing else can.** A dev server that outlives its session reparents itself to launchd and keeps nothing pointing back at the terminal that started it, so no tool on your Mac can tell you it is yours. Wortel can, because the working directory still answers days later. Two were sitting on the development machine while this was written: a wrangler four days old holding 399 MB, and a bun script five days old. Neither was serving anything reachable. They appear under "Not attached to a session", with how long they have been there and which project they came out of.

**Closing a session says what it will stop.** The warning used to read "A process is still running in this session", which is equally true of a session holding one `sleep` and one holding 1.4 GB across two dev servers. Now it names them: "Still running: bun dev:web (:3000), claude, bun dev:images. Closing stops 8 processes and frees 1.05 GB." The agent is named too, because closing the session does stop it, and that is the part worth reading.

Your agent is never offered a Quit button, and neither is the session's own shell. What counts as one job is the process group, which is how shells actually work rather than a guess about which processes belong together.

**Notifications carry the session's current name.** A notification was posted with the session's name and the session was often renamed moments later, when the on-device namer or the agent's own title came back a few seconds after the turn ended. The banner then said "Session 3" while the sidebar said what the work actually was, which reads as a notification about some other session. A renamed session now rewrites its own banner in place, without a second ding.

**Two ways a terminal could come back blank.** A session restored from a previous launch could fail to take the keyboard, leaving a window that draws but will not accept a keystroke, with nothing on screen saying why. And a session whose program sat idle while the app was closed had nothing to replay and nothing to prompt a repaint, so it came back empty until you touched it. Both are fixed.

**The git panel can pull.** Fetch told you that you were behind and then left you to go and type the obvious command. Pull sits between fetch and push, and is fast-forward only: a panel with no conflict UI has no business starting a merge it cannot finish, so a diverged branch gets git's own refusal and the terminal is right there to resolve it in.

**Image diffs say what the change costs.** An image commit is reviewed on two things, what changed and what it weighs, and the caption only ever had the first. A PNG that gained 400 KB without visibly changing looked like no change at all. Both sides now show their size alongside their dimensions.

**Worktree sessions stop inventing a folder.** A session's folder is the part of its name before the last slash, so naming a worktree session after its branch filed it under a folder named for the branch's namespace: a second sidebar group holding exactly the sessions that belonged in the first one. New sessions are named for the branch's leaf, and any session already filed in a phantom folder moves back on this launch.

Two new anonymous events come with this release, both counts only: that the running list was opened, with rough numbers of jobs and of processes that had outlived their session, and that something was quit from it. Never what they are, what they are serving, or where they are running. The full list is on [the privacy page](/privacy), which remains the whole list.
