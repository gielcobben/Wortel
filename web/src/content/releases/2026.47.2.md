---
date: 2026-11-18
title: New sessions can start your agent, and Wortel has Settings
summary: Tell Wortel what a new session starts with, like claude, and every new session opens with it. That lives in a short new Settings window (⌘,). Agents you have left idle out of sight now give their memory back, and come back when you select their session.
---

**New sessions start your agent.** In Settings, type any command into "New sessions start with", or pick one of the agents Wortel finds on your Mac from the menu beside the field. Every new session then starts it: ⌘N, ⌥⌘N, ⇧⌘N, ⌘O, a folder's + and a folder dropped on the sidebar. Session ▸ New Terminal (⌃⌘N) opens one without it, and is there only while the field is filled. Left empty, a new session is a plain terminal, as before. `wortel new` and the sessions Wortel brings back at launch don't use it.

**Settings.** Wortel ▸ Settings… (⌘,) opens a small window with two tabs. General has what new sessions start with, whether Source Control shows its commit box, and whether local servers open in your browser. Updates has automatic updates and anonymous usage data. The menu items Check for Updates Automatically, Share Anonymous Usage Data and Open Servers in Default Browser are gone; their switches are here now. The ⓘ at the bottom of the sidebar still has its usage data switch.

**New sessions go on top.** A new session outside a folder now goes to the top of the sidebar, so the newest is first. In a folder it goes under the session you're on.

**Idle agents give their memory back.** An idle Claude Code holds 130 to 400 MB. When one has finished its turn and its session hasn't been on screen for an hour, Wortel now ends it and keeps the shell. Select the session and the same conversation is resumed, in a second or two. One waiting on your answer, with something still running under it, with a prompt typed and not sent, or with Remote Control on, gets eight hours instead, and what it started ends with it. A working agent and the session in front of you are never touched. Other agents are left alone for now.

**Terminals use less memory.** A terminal you haven't looked at for ten minutes gives back its screen's memory, about 70 MB each, and has it back when you select it. A leak that grew Wortel by over 100 MB an hour is fixed.

**Faster switching.** A click on a session switches on mouse-down, as Finder's sidebar does, and its terminal appears at its final size instead of growing into place.

**Smaller things.** The sidebar's toggle and arrows move with it when it opens or closes. A terminal drawn under the side column or in a corner of its pane is now put right as soon as the layout settles. Usage data now counts whether a new session started with a command and which agent was ended or resumed this way, with nothing about the command you typed, the session or the conversation.
