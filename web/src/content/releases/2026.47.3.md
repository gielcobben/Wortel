---
date: 2026-11-20
title: More than one window, and terminals that come back as you left them
summary: Wortel can open more than one window, and from now on every session's screen and scrollback come back exactly as they were after you quit or update. Claude Code and Pi sessions already running when you update won't show when they're working until you restart them.
---

**More than one window.** Window ▸ New Window (⇧⌘N) opens a second window with a sidebar of its own. Drag a session onto the other window's sidebar to move it there, or out of the window to give it one of its own; it keeps running as it is. A session's menu has Move to New Window and Move to Window, and Window ▸ Merge All Windows puts everything back in one. Closing a window closes its sessions, asking first if something is running in them. New Worktree Session is ⌥⇧⌘N now, since ⇧⌘N opens a window.

**Your terminals come back as you left them.** After you quit or update, a session's terminal used to start empty and its agent printed what it could again, sometimes narrow or twice over. Every session's screen and scrollback are now kept when Wortel quits and put back when it opens, before anything else. It starts with the first quit after this update; the update itself comes back the old way. After a crash, the shells are still there and the agent draws its screen again, as before.

**Restart agents that were already running.** Claude Code and Pi sessions already running when you update won't show when they're working until you restart them. They ask whether the terminal is listening only when they start.

**Agents say what they're doing.** Claude Code, Codex, Pi, opencode and others now tell Wortel when they're working, waiting for your approval, asking a question, finished or failed, and Wortel no longer guesses from the title or the screen. A Codex turn that ended no longer arrives as "needs input".

**Smoother, steadier terminals.** Resizing the window or dragging the divider keeps up with the pointer. A terminal no longer ends up under the side column after you switch sessions, a session you come back to after a while is no longer drawn in scattered pieces, and agents out of sight work at the width you'll see them at.

**The sidebar sorts.** Each section has Sort By in its "…": Recent Activity, Date Created, Name, or by hand. Folders come before loose sessions, section headers are easier to read, and pressing the session you're in puts the keyboard back in its terminal.

**Smaller things.** ⌘-clicking a path an agent printed opens it in Wortel at its line. A new agent session that would start in your home folder gets a folder of its own in ~/Wortel, and sessions opened at home are called Home. Every terminal follows light and dark at once, and text stays readable when you switch. ⌘O and renaming a folder work again.

**Usage data.** Wortel now counts when a window is opened, with roughly how many are open, and when sessions move between windows, and how. It still never collects what you type, what your terminals show, or the names of your files, folders or repositories.
